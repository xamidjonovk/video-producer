# Directing the first cut of a speech-led Reel

Use the approved spoken take as the timeline. For a revision, open the current edit and notes, carry forward approved words, pronunciation, voice, tempo and visuals, and mark the timecodes affected by the request. If the user wants to approve the words before recording, stop at the script. Once approved, cover visual changes by editing the picture under the existing voice.

## Build coverage against the audio

Listen once for the argument and again for the spoken landmarks. Add a row to the job's `script.json` scenes for each change of idea. Record in/out times on the final audio, exact spoken cue, what the viewer should understand, shot source and source timecode, visible action, and what must remain readable on the cut. Include the face windows if the presenter is on camera. Render from the job's edit plan or motion composition. The scene rows are editorial notes.

| When the voice introduces… | Make this happen on screen | Cut when… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A person's claim | Open on an expressive, steady take, or a close-up of the problem already in progress | The next phrase asks for evidence |
| A named app or search | Capture its actual interface. In Obsidian, type a relevant query, select a matching note, and follow a link in the note. Crop to keep the query and result legible. Label an illustrative reconstruction onscreen if real UI is unavailable. | The viewer has seen the result of the click |
| Several parts of a system | Establish the whole diagram, move the camera close once, then illuminate each part on its spoken name. Keep earlier parts as dim context. | The final part completes the pattern |
| A change of mind | Keep the old conclusion visible, introduce one dated piece of evidence, trace its connection, then replace the conclusion. Hold the changed words. | A viewer can identify what changed and why |
| An abstract claim with a real-world consequence | Choose owned or licensed footage of the relevant action, e.g. a buyer comparing two quotes. Note the source, license or ownership and in/out points in the job. | The action answers the line |

## Animate the explanation

Choose a visual rule for the sequence. A folder can open and reveal a note, the note's link can become an edge in a diagram, and that edge can carry new evidence. In HyperFrames time each reveal, draw, focus shift and hold against a spoken marker. Keep changing the evidence or the point of view as the narration advances. For an intentionally quiet beat, an eye movement or a hand finishing a real action can carry the shot.

Give the eye one target at a time. Use depth when it reveals a relationship, for example moving a layer forward so its connections become visible. On a request for a brain in empty space, a dark field can carry the whole sequence. Borrow pacing, easing and clarity from references, and create original artwork. Keep text inside the target platform's visible area and read the smallest meaningful label in a phone-size preview. Change timing or framing where the viewer loses the subject. Let results settle before the next cut.

## Keep the face and the mix believable

For a cloned presenter, compare contact sheets across the supplied takes, then play candidates at normal speed. Choose a continuous performance with a compatible delivery, clear mouth and restrained head motion. Inspect for overlays, obstructions and unstable crops. Mark the exact face windows on the locked narration. For each paid take use the matching clean audio excerpt at its final tempo, check the live quote and existing receipts, and respect the scope of the approval. Assemble the accepted picture over the continuous narration master. Choose another source moment or cover a weak join with a relevant insert. [Presenter preparation](presenter-clone.md)

Start the mix with speech alone. Add a brief click at a selection or a soft impact when new evidence lands. Introduce a music bed only where it serves the brief, and audition it under the softest actual words. Listen on a small speaker or in mono. Reduce, duck or remove any layer that masks consonants. Measure the encoded master and record what you actually heard. [Audio and color finishing](finishing.md)

## Review at delivery size

- Play the opening, every face window and both sides of each transition with sound at normal speed. Inspect visible lip closures, pauses and drift, including the end of the take. Matching audio timestamps alone cannot establish mouth sync.
- Watch the cut muted at phone size. Can you recognize the product, follow the action and name the changed conclusion? Replace decorative movement or generic stock wherever the picture loses the argument.
- Play the whole export once, check the first and last spoken words, caption timing and sound effects, then run the technical checks. Document any playback check you could not perform. Deliver the MP4, requested captions and editable sources with the job. [Delivery checks](quality.md)
